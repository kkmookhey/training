## Two days. One target. Three verbs.

**Build → Break → Secure**, every module.

<!-- Notes: This is the rhythm for all 8 modules. You'll stand up a vulnerable layer, exploit it, then flip a single security flag and watch the exploit die. The diff between vulnerable and secure IS the lesson. -->

---

## AI isn't one thing you attack.

It's a **supply chain**.

<!-- Notes: The core reframe. People come in thinking "prompt injection" is the topic. It's one attack on one layer. Real systems are a chain of components, each trusting the last, each adding attack surface. We attack the chain, in order. -->

---

## The spine

```
model → RAG → tools/agents → MCP → multi-agent → production
```

Each layer adds attack surface. The OWASP LLM Top 10 lands where it lives.

<!-- Notes: Draw this as a horizontal stack building left to right. This is the map for the entire course. We are NOT teaching OWASP as a checklist — we walk the chain and the vulnerabilities appear where they actually occur. Point out that this mirrors a real engagement. -->

## How you know you won

Pass/fail is a **server-side audit log** —
not what the model *said*.

<!-- Notes: Critical, and reassuring. LLMs are non-deterministic; we never grade on the model's words. Every objective is validated by a mechanism query: did the exploit actually fire? This is why the labs are reliable and repeatable. You'll see /validate return core: pass. -->

---

## Rules of the range

- **One build, one flag.** `vulnerable` ↔ `secure`.
- **Reset** any module, any time.
- **Core** objective for everyone · **stretch** for fast finishers.

<!-- Notes: No difficulty ladder — a floor and a ceiling. If you break something, reset. If you finish early, there's always a stretch. Day 1 is keyless (local model); Day 2 uses your own API key. -->

## The attacker mindset

You're not "prompting."
You're supplying **untrusted input that becomes instructions.**

<!-- Notes: The one idea to carry all week. Every layer we attack has a boundary where data crosses into instruction — the prompt, a retrieved doc, a tool description, another agent's message. Find that boundary, own the system. -->
